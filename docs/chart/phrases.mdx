title: "Phrases"
description: "Star power, solos, lanes and drum fills in a .chart file."
---

A phrase marks a stretch of one track, such as a star power phrase or a solo. It goes in an instrument section, with the notes it covers, and applies to that section's instrument and difficulty:

```text
[ExpertSingle]
{
  768 = N 0 0
  768 = S 2 1536
  960 = N 1 0
  1152 = N 2 0
  2304 = N 3 0
  3072 = N 4 0
  3072 = E solo
  6144 = N 0 0
  6144 = E soloend
}
```

Here a star power phrase covers the notes from tick 768 up to tick 2304, and a solo covers the notes from tick 3072 through tick 6144.

Most phrases are `S` events:

```text
<tick> = S <type> <length>
```

The length is in ticks, and the phrase ends at its end tick, `tick + length`:

| Type | Phrase | Instruments | Covers its end tick |
|---|---|---|---|
| `2` | [Star power](#star-power) | All | No |
| `64` | [Drum fill](#drum-fills) | Drums. On 5-fret, only as a Big Rock Ending | Yes |
| `65` | [Lane](#lanes): a single roll lane on drums, a tremolo lane on 5-fret | Drums and 5-fret | Yes |
| `66` | [Lane](#lanes): a double roll lane on drums, a trill lane on 5-fret | Drums and 5-fret | Yes |

A [solo](#solos) is written as two text events instead, and covers both of its ticks.

## Rules for every phrase

- **Required:** at least one note in each phrase.
- **Recommended:** a length of at least one tick for each phrase.

**Edge case: a phrase of length 0.** It covers the notes on its tick.

## Star power

A star power phrase marks notes the player can earn star power from: hitting every note in it fills a quarter of the star power meter.

```text
768 = S 2 1536
```

Neither game adds star power to a chart that has none.

## Solos

A solo is marked by two text events, `solo` and `soloend`, written without quotation marks. It covers its notes from the `solo` tick through the `soloend` tick, both included:

```text
3072 = E solo
6144 = E soloend
```

Each `solo` has its `soloend` before the next `solo`.

## Drum fills

A drum fill marks a stretch where the drummer can play freely and activate star power by hitting the fill's last note. It includes its end tick. An exporter should put a note on the fill's end tick.

**Edge case: a drum fill without a note on its end tick.** The games choose different notes to activate star power. Clone Hero uses the note closest to the end tick, from the fill's start up to `resolution ÷ 32 + 1` ticks after its end, and the fill activates nothing without a note there. YARG uses the first note at or after the end tick, however far after the fill it is, or the track's last note.

**Edge case: a drums track without drum fills.** The games add their own. Clone Hero adds a star power activation every four measures. YARG adds drum fills when the track has star power.

A drum fill whose tick is at or after the [`coda`](/chart/events#the-coda) is a Big Rock Ending instead: a closing freestyle section with no star power activation.

On 5-fret guitar, bass and keys, an `S 64` at or after the `coda` is a Big Rock Ending, and has no meaning before it.

**Edge case: where a Big Rock Ending ends.** A drum fill covers its end tick, but Clone Hero's Big Rock Ending doesn't: a note on its end tick comes after it. **Recommended:** a note after each Big Rock Ending, which closes it, and the Big Rock Ending ending before that note. From its code, YARG gives a Big Rock Ending's bonus only when the player hits the note that closes it.

{/* review: Q-bre
A Big Rock Ending is a closing freestyle section. From its code, YARG collects a bonus during it and banks the bonus only when the player hits the note after it, so without one there's no bonus. Clone Hero gives no Big Rock Ending bonus, and needs no note after it. GuitarGame_ChartFormats requires at least one note after it. The page only recommended ending a Big Rock Ending before the note that closes it, which assumes there is one. Must a note follow a Big Rock Ending?
- A. Required | "Required: a note after each Big Rock Ending." An invalid form says that without one YARG gives no bonus | A new invalid form for a score bonus in one game, and a scan-chart check for it

- C. Leave the recommendation as it was | No change | A reader may miss that YARG's bonus needs the note

*/}

**Edge case: Big Rock Endings on 5-fret.** Clone Hero and YARG play a `.chart` Big Rock Ending only on drums, so no game plays one on 5-fret guitar, bass or keys from a `.chart` file.

## Lanes

A lane marks a passage too fast to play note for note. Inside it, the player keeps up the motion at their own speed, and each note counts as hit. A lane includes its end tick, and lanes may overlap.

- **On drums,** `S 65` is a roll on one pad, and `S 66` a roll that alternates between two pads. The lane takes its pads from the notes it covers, and never includes the kick. **Recommended:** a roll on one pad starts with a note on one pad, apart from a kick. When it starts on a chord, Clone Hero takes the chord's lowest pad, and YARG the pad of its first note.
- **On 5-fret guitar, bass and keys,** `S 65` is a tremolo lane: one fret or chord repeated, at least two notes, strummed freely. `S 66` is a trill lane: two single frets alternating, at least three notes, played freely between them. These are what `.mid` notes 126 and 127 mean.

A lane should cover at least two notes, and only notes that fit its pattern: YARG cancels a lane whose notes don't.

{/* review: Q-roll
A roll on one pad (S 65, .mid 126) takes its pad from the notes it covers. When its first note is a chord, the games pick different pads: Clone Hero the chord's lowest pad, and YARG the pad of the chord's first note. GuitarGame_ChartFormats says to use the next single note's pad, which neither game does. No hash depends on it. Should the spec say anything?
- A. Leave it out | No text | A roll that starts on a chord can show on different pads in each game
- B. A recommendation for charters, as written now | Start a roll on one pad with a note on one pad, apart from a kick | One sentence on each Phrases page. No game changes, and charts that don't follow it stay valid
- C. GuitarGame_ChartFormats' rule as the standard | The next single note's pad, proposed to both games | Two game changes for a rare chart, and a rule a game must look ahead to apply
Recommendation: B. It keeps charts away from the case where the games disagree, without asking either game to change.
*/}

**Edge case: lanes Clone Hero skips.** Clone Hero doesn't play a drum lane that covers only one pad note, or whose first pad note comes more than a sixteenth of a beat after its start: 12 ticks at a resolution of 192.

**Edge case: lanes on 5-fret.** YARG plays tremolo and trill lanes only from a `.mid` file, and Clone Hero plays lanes only on drums, so no game plays them from a `.chart` file. On 6-fret, `S 64` to `S 66` have no meaning.

## Ignored

- **A phrase of any other type,** and a phrase on an instrument the table doesn't list for it. YARG reads `S 67` as a kick lane on drums.
- **Versus phrases,** `S 0` and `S 1`, which mark the parts each player plays in a face-off in older games. An exporter should keep them when rewriting a file.

## Invalid forms

<Accordion title="Two phrases of one type at one tick">
Clone Hero and scan-chart use the longer phrase, and YARG the one written later. scan-chart keeps both when saving.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Overlapping phrases of one type, other than lanes">
Clone Hero and scan-chart cut the earlier phrase where the later starts, and extend the later one to whichever ends last. YARG gives each note to the latest phrase that started at or before it, so the rest of a longer earlier phrase is lost. scan-chart keeps the phrases as written when saving.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Nested solos">
At each `soloend`, every scanner ends the most recent `solo` that hasn't ended. The two solos then overlap, and each scanner reads them as overlapping phrases of one type. scan-chart keeps them as written when saving.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="A phrase that covers no notes">
A scanner ignores it. scan-chart reports an empty star power phrase, solo or lane, and `fixIssues` removes it.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="A soloend with no open solo, or a solo that never ends">
A scanner ignores it.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="A solo in square brackets, such as E [solo]">
Clone Hero and scan-chart don't read `E [solo]` and `E [soloend]` as a solo, and YARG does. scan-chart keeps them as text, and writes them back.
</Accordion>
