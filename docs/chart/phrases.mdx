---
title: "Phrases"
description: "Star power, solos, roll lanes and drum fills in a .chart file."
---

A phrase marks a stretch of one track, such as a star power phrase or a solo. It applies to the instrument and difficulty of the section that holds it. Most phrases are `S` events:

```text
<tick> = S <type> <length>
```

The length is in ticks. A phrase covers the notes from its tick up to, but not including, its end tick, `tick + length`. Some phrases include their end tick, as their descriptions below say.

| Type | Phrase | Instruments |
|---|---|---|
| `2` | Star power | All |
| `64` | Drum fill | Drums |
| `65` | Single roll lane | Drums |
| `66` | Double roll lane | Drums |
| `0`, `1` | Versus phrase, for player 1 or player 2 | All |

A reader ignores a phrase of any other type, and a phrase on an instrument the table doesn't list for it.

## Rules for every phrase

- A track must not hold two phrases of one type at one tick. A reader that finds two uses the longer.
- Phrases of one type must not overlap. A reader that finds two overlapping cuts the earlier one where the later starts, and extends the later one to whichever ends last.
- A phrase should cover at least one note. A reader ignores a phrase that covers none.
- A phrase of length 0 covers the notes on its tick. A writer should give every phrase a length.

## Star power

A star power phrase marks notes the player can earn star power from: hitting every note in it fills the star power meter.

```text
768 = S 2 1536
```

## Solos

A solo is marked by two text events, `solo` and `soloend`, written without quotation marks. It covers its notes from the `solo` tick through the `soloend` tick, both included:

```text
3072 = E solo
6144 = E soloend
```

- A `soloend` ends the most recent `solo` that hasn't ended. Solos must not be nested.
- A reader ignores a `soloend` with no open `solo`, and a `solo` that never ends.

## Drum fills

A drum fill marks a stretch where the drummer can play freely and activate star power by hitting the fill's last note. It includes its end tick. A writer should put a note on the fill's end tick.

A drum fill whose tick is at or after the [`coda`](/chart/events#the-coda) is a Big Rock Ending instead: a closing freestyle section with no star power activation.

## Roll lanes

A roll lane marks a stretch the player can play as a roll instead of note by note. It includes its end tick.

- **A single roll lane** (`S 65`) covers a roll on one pad.
- **A double roll lane** (`S 66`) covers a roll that alternates between two pads.

The lane takes its pads from the notes it covers, and never includes the kick. A lane should cover at least two notes. Lanes may overlap.

## Versus phrases

Versus phrases mark the parts each player plays in a face-off, from older games. Most readers ignore them, and a writer should keep them when rewriting a file.
