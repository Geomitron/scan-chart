---
title: "[Events]"
description: "Practice sections, lyrics and other song-wide text events in a .chart file."
---

`[Events]` holds the text events that apply to the whole song: practice sections, lyrics and the song's end. Each is an `E` event with its text in quotation marks:

```text
[Events]
{
  0 = E "section Intro"
  768 = E "phrase_start"
  768 = E "lyric Hel-"
  864 = E "lyric lo"
  1152 = E "phrase_end"
  3072 = E "section Verse 1"
  15360 = E "end"
}
```

A reader removes spaces and tabs around the text, and one pair of square brackets around the whole text, so `E "[section Intro]"` means the same as `E "section Intro"`. A writer should write neither. A reader matches every event below in the letter case shown, and ignores any other text in `[Events]`.

## Practice sections

A practice section marks where a part of the song starts, such as the intro or a verse. It runs until the next practice section:

```text
<tick> = E "section <name>"
```

- A space or `_` separates `section` from the name. The name must not be empty. A reader removes spaces at the ends of the name.
- `prc` is an older word for `section`: `prc_verse_1` is a practice section named `verse_1`. A writer should write `section`.
- A tick must not hold two practice sections. A reader that finds two uses the later one.
- A chart should have at least one practice section.

## Lyrics

A lyric is one syllable or word of the vocals. Vocal phrases group lyrics into the lines a reader shows:

```text
<tick> = E "lyric <text>"
<tick> = E "phrase_start"
<tick> = E "phrase_end"
```

- **A lyric** is `lyric`, a space, and the text. A reader ignores a lyric with no text.
- **A phrase** runs from a `phrase_start` to the next `phrase_start` or `phrase_end`. It holds the lyrics from its start tick through its end tick.
- **A lyric on a tick where one phrase ends and another starts** belongs to the phrase that starts there.
- **On a tick with both**, a writer must write `phrase_end` before `phrase_start`. A reader reads them in the order written.
- **The last phrase** must end with a `phrase_end`. A reader ignores a phrase that never ends.
- **A lyric outside every phrase** should not be written. A reader ignores it.
- **A `phrase_end` with no open phrase** has no effect.

A `.chart` file holds one set of lyrics, without pitches.

## The end of the song

```text
<tick> = E "end"
```

`end` marks where the song ends. A chart should have at most one, at or after the end of its last note, sustain included. A reader that finds several uses the first. It ignores an `end` that comes before the last note ends.

## The coda

```text
<tick> = E "coda"
```

`coda` marks where the song's big ending starts. A drum fill at or after it is a Big Rock Ending: see [Drum fills](/chart/phrases#drum-fills). A chart should have at most one `coda`. A reader that finds several uses the first.
